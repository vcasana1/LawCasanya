\begin{abstract}
We study a finite, deterministic completion problem for the M\"obius function.  At scale $N$, the non-squarefree support is fixed, selected prime signs may be fixed, and the remaining prime signs are treated adversarially.  The endpoint envelope
\[
 B_N(\mathcal I)=\max_{x\in\mathcal C_N(\mathcal I)}
 \left|\sum_{n\le N}x_n\right|
\]
separates the amount of information supplied from the worst endpoint still compatible with that information.  The first arithmetic layers admit exact closed forms: unrestricted squarefree signs and free multiplicative prime signs both have envelope $Q(N)$, while fixing the sign at $2$ gives
\[
 B_N=Q_{\rm odd}(N)-Q_{\rm odd}(N/2)\sim \frac{2}{\pi^2}N.
\]
Thus a very large reduction in the number of completions need not change the endpoint exponent.

The second part of the paper develops a finite combinatorial representation of short-correlation comparisons.  For
\[
 C_h(x)=\sum_{n\le N-h}x_nx_{n+h},\qquad
 d_h(x)=C_h(x)-C_h(\mu),
\]
the discrepancy set $D(x)=\{n:x_n\ne\mu(n)\}$ defines a cut in a signed displacement graph $G_h$.  Exactly,
\[
 \frac{d_h(x)}2=\Cut_{G_h}(D(x)).
\]
Because admissible discrepancy sets arise from prime-sign assignments, they form the image of a binary linear code.  Each displacement edge yields a parity form $q\cdot b$, and its sign is determined by the parity identity
\[
 -\mu(n)\mu(n+h)=(-1)^{|q|+h+1}.
\]
Hence every first-witness condition is an explicit threshold of low-order parity tests on the free prime-sign cube.  An exhaustive computation at $N=110$ over $2^{28}$ completions gives $9{,}311{,}936$ strict square-root excursions, a survivor chain ending in $13\to2\to0$ at lags $9,10,11$, and two hardest states with first witness $h_*=11$.  Complete signed-cut data for all $109$ lags are recorded.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and notation}

Let
\[
 M(N)=\sum_{n\le N}\mu(n)
\]
and let
\[
 Q(N)=\#\{n\le N:\mu(n)\ne0\}.
\]
The finite program begins with a family of words
\[
 x=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in\{-1,0,1\}^N
\]
and progressively imposes exact arithmetic information.  If $\mathcal I$ denotes the declared information and closure rules, write
\[
 \mathcal C_N(\mathcal I)
 =\{x:\ x\text{ is compatible with }\mathcal I\}
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 B_N(\mathcal I)=
 \max_{x\in\mathcal C_N(\mathcal I)}
 \left|\sum_{n\le N}x_n\right|.
 }
\]
The object is deterministic: a single surviving completion matters, irrespective of its proportion inside the state space.

The strict square-root excursion class is
\[
 \boxed{\RHC_N=\{x:|S_x(N)|>\sqrt N\}},
 \qquad
 S_x(N)=\sum_{n\le N}x_n.
\]
For $\delta\ge0$ define
\[
 \boxed{
 \RHF_{N,\delta}
 =\{x:|S_x(N)|>N^{1/2+\delta}\},
 \qquad
 \RHF_{N,0}=\RHC_N.
 }
\]
The distinction is finite and exact.  In particular, the false Mertens inequality
$|M(N)|\le\sqrt N$ cannot hold for every $N$, whereas the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to
\[
 M(N)=O_\varepsilon(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})
 \qquad(\forall\,\varepsilon>0).
\]

\section{Literature boundary}

The neighboring literature is substantial, but it addresses different objects.

Vandehey introduced $P$-multiplicative functions, imposing
$f(pn)=f(p)f(n)$ only for primes in a prescribed finite set $P$, and studied bounded partial sums in that weakened multiplicative setting \cite{Vandehey2012}.  Thus finite prime prescriptions and partial multiplicativity are established objects.

Aymone and Sidoravicius study random multiplicative functions supported on the squarefree integers whose prime values are independent $\{\pm1\}$ variables biased toward the M\"obius signs, including an RH-equivalent probabilistic statement for a family of such models \cite{AymoneSidoravicius2017}.  Koukoulopoulos and Soundararajan prove inverse structural results for multiplicative functions with very small partial sums \cite{KoukSound2020}.  Matom\"aki, Radziwi{\l}{\l}, and Tao establish local sign-pattern results for Liouville and M\"obius \cite{MRT2016}.  These works delimit the classical multiplicative-function side of the problem.

Aperiodic autocorrelation of finite binary sequences is also classical; Willms, for example, relates the full autocorrelation vector to run structure \cite{Willms2013}.  Signed graphs and signed cuts form a mature graph-theoretic framework \cite{Zaslavsky1982,Gallier2016}.  Consequently, neither ``autocorrelation'' nor ``signed cut'' by itself constitutes a novelty claim.

The specific finite object isolated below is more restrictive: a simultaneous family of signed displacement graphs whose edge signs are inherited from the finite M\"obius word, with cuts restricted to discrepancy sets generated by squarefree multiplicative prime-sign completions.  Targeted searches using combinations of M\"obius autocorrelation, signed cuts, displacement graphs, multiplicative completions, and first-lag witnesses did not locate this exact formulation.  This negative search is a bibliographic boundary, not a theorem of novelty.

For computational context, Hurst computed all values of $M(x)$ through $10^{16}$ and later isolated values through $10^{25}$ \cite{Hurst2018,Hurst2026}.  Those computations emphasize the distinction between computing the true M\"obius word and exhaustively solving a completion problem over exponentially many compatible words.

\section{Exact endpoint envelopes for the first arithmetic layers}

\subsection{Squarefree support and free multiplicativity}

\begin{proposition}
Let the nonsquarefree coordinates be fixed to zero and every squarefree coordinate be free in $\{\pm1\}$.  Then
\[
 \boxed{B_N^{\sqf}=Q(N)}.
\]
If instead the squarefree word is required to be multiplicative and all prime signs are free, then
\[
 \boxed{B_N^{\mult}=Q(N)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The squarefree-support maximum is attained by assigning $+1$ to every squarefree coordinate.  Under multiplicativity the same word is obtained by setting every prime sign to $+1$; every squarefree value is then $+1$.
\end{proof}

Since
\[
 Q(N)\sim\frac{6}{\pi^2}N,
\]
both envelopes have exponent one.  Multiplicativity can therefore collapse the state count from exponential in $Q(N)$ to exponential in $\pi(N)$ without changing the extremal envelope.

\subsection{Fixing the sign at $2$}

Let $f$ be squarefree-supported and multiplicative, with
\[
 f(2)=-1,\qquad f(p)=\sigma_p\in\{\pm1\}\quad(p\ge3).
\]

\begin{theorem}
For every $N$,
\[
 \boxed{
 B_N^{\mu(2)}
 =Q_{\rm odd}(N)-Q_{\rm odd}(N/2)
 =Q_{\rm odd}(N)-Q_{\rm even}(N).
 }
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 B_N^{\mu(2)}\sim\frac{2}{\pi^2}N.
 }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every odd squarefree $m\le N/2$ is paired with $2m$, and
\[
 f(2m)=f(2)f(m)=-f(m).
\]
These pairs cancel.  Hence
\[
 S_f(N)=
 \sum_{\substack{N/2<m\le N\\m\ {\rm odd,\ squarefree}}}f(m).
\]
All remaining terms are simultaneously made $+1$ by choosing the odd-prime signs appropriately, so the maximum is their count.  Finally, the even squarefree integers up to $N$ are in bijection with odd squarefree $m\le N/2$ via $m\mapsto2m$.  The standard densities are
\[
 Q_{\rm odd}(N)\sim\frac4{\pi^2}N,\qquad
 Q_{\rm even}(N)\sim\frac2{\pi^2}N.
\]
\end{proof}

Thus the finite drop
\[
 16\longrightarrow6\qquad(N=25)
\]
is the finite manifestation of the constant change
\[
 \frac6{\pi^2}\longrightarrow\frac2{\pi^2},
\]
an asymptotic factor of three, not an exponent contraction.

If
\[
 B_N=cN^\theta,
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 E_N:=\frac{\log B_N}{\log N}-\frac12
 =\theta-\frac12+\frac{\log c}{\log N}.
 }
\]
Thus $E_N$ contains a large finite-size bias when $c\ne1$.  The appropriate scaling diagnostics are
\[
 R_N=\frac{B_N}{N}
\]
and a local log--log slope
\[
 \boxed{
 \theta_N^{(a)}
 =\frac{\log B_{aN}-\log B_N}{\log a}.
 }
\]

The two following models are not interchangeable.

\subsection{Model A: prescribed prime signs without residual propagation}

Revealed prime signs are fixed and unrevealed prime signs remain independent.  No primality statement is inferred from a residual factor.

\begin{proposition}[Prime-singleton obstruction]
Suppose $k$ unrevealed primes lie in $(N/2,N]$.  Then
\[
 B_N\ge k.
\]
If the true M\"obius completion is present in the admissible set, then
\[
 \boxed{B_N\ge 2k-|M(N)|}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each such prime occurs only at its own coordinate.  After the other variables are fixed,
\[
 S_N=C+\sum_{p\in P_{\rm free}}\sigma_p,
\]
so the maximum over these signs is $|C|+k\ge k$.  Starting from the true word and flipping all $k$ prime signs changes the endpoint by $2k$, whence the second inequality.
\end{proof}

If all upper-half primes are free,
\[
 k=\pi(N)-\pi(N/2)\sim\frac{N}{2\log N},
\]
so this model retains an $N^{1-o(1)}$ endpoint obstruction.

\subsection{Model B: residual closure by $P_r$}

Let
\[
 P_r=\{p_1,\ldots,p_r\}.
\]
Strip known prime factors from $n$.  Repeated known factors force zero; residual $1$ fixes the sign; a residual $q\le p_r^2$ with no factor in $P_r$ is forced prime; larger unresolved residual cores are shared ternary variables.

At $N=25$:

The endpoint has the exact form
\[
 S_N=A_r(N)+\sum_c w_{r,N}(c)z_c,
\qquad z_c\in\{-1,0,1\},
\]
with
\[
 \boxed{
 w_{r,N}(c)=
 \sum_{\substack{d\mid P_r^\#\\dc\le N}}\mu(d).
 }
\]
Hence
\[
 \boxed{
 B_r^E(N)=|A_r(N)|+\sum_c|w_{r,N}(c)|
 }
\]
and the endpoint distribution is encoded by
\[
 x^{A_r(N)}\prod_c(x^{-w_c}+1+x^{w_c}).
\]

Every unresolved core
\[
 c>\max(N/2,p_r^2),\qquad(c,P_r^\#)=1

appears in only one endpoint coordinate.  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 B_{N,P_r}\ge

 }

\end{proposition}

\section{Three exact combinatorial barriers}

\subsection{A terminal global identity}

Let $f$ be squarefree-supported and multiplicative, with $f(p)\in\{\pm1\}$.  If
\[
 \sum_{d\le N}f(d)\left\lfloor\frac Nd\right\rfloor=1,
\]
then, writing $g=\mathbf1*f$,
\[
 g(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}(1+f(p))\ge0
\]
and
\[
 \sum_{n\le N}g(n)=1.
\]
Since $g(1)=1$, one obtains $g(n)=0$ for every $2\le n\le N$, hence
\[
 f(p)=-1\qquad(p\le N).
\]
Thus this identity is terminal in the prime-sign model: it determines the entire prime-sign prefix.

\subsection{Support-transport lemma}

\begin{lemma}
Let $U$ be a target coordinate set and let $A_j$ be constraint rows.  Suppose
\[
 d_U=\max_j|\operatorname{supp}(A_j)\cap U|.
\]
If a dual combination $y$ uses at most $s$ rows, then
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathbf1_U-(A^Ty)_U\|_1\ge |U|-sd_U.
 }
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The union of the selected row supports covers at most $sd_U$ coordinates of $U$.  At every uncovered coordinate, $(A^Ty)_u=0$, so the residual against $\mathbf1_U$ contributes exactly one.
\end{proof}

For divisor-local rows on the upper-half squarefree set, $d_U=1$.  For pointwise translational rows of bounded width $h$, $d_U=O(h)$.  This is a coverage obstruction; it does not apply to genuinely global rows.

\subsection{Aggregated divisor identities and inversion}

From
\[
 \sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=\delta_{n1},
\]
multiply by arbitrary coefficients $a_n$ and aggregate:
\[
 \sum_{d\le N}\mu(d)\sum_{m\le N/d}a_{dm}=a_1.
\]
Put
\[
 c_d=\sum_{m\le N/d}a_{dm}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 M(N)=a_1+\sum_{d\le N}\mu(d)(1-c_d)
 }
\]
and
\[
 |M(N)|\le |a_1|
 +\sum_{\substack{d\le N\\d\ {\rm squarefree}}}|1-c_d|.
\]
The transform is exactly invertible:
\[
 \boxed{
 a_n=\sum_{k\le N/n}\mu(k)c_{nk}.
 }
\]
In particular, the exact target $c_d\equiv1$ gives
\[
 a_n=M(\lfloor N/n\rfloor),
 \qquad
 a_1=M(N).
\]
If $a_n=0$ for $n>K$, then $c_d=0$ for $d>K$, and therefore the squarefree $\ell^1$ defect is at least
\[
 Q(N)-Q(K).
\]

\section{The fixed-$\sigma_2$ multiplicative cube}

From now on,
\[
 x_1=1,\qquad
 x_n=0\quad(n\text{ nonsquarefree}),
\]
and, for squarefree $n$,
\[
 \boxed{
 x_n=\prod_{p\mid n}\sigma_p,
 \qquad
 \sigma_2=-1,\quad
 \sigma_p\in\{\pm1\}\ (p\ge3).
 }
\]
Thus
\[
 |\Omega_N|=2^{\pi(N)-1}.
\]
The true M\"obius word is the all-minus prime assignment.

At $N=25$,
\[
 \boxed{
 S_{25}
 =\sigma_{13}+\sigma_{17}+\sigma_{19}+\sigma_{23}
 +\sigma_3\sigma_5+\sigma_3\sigma_7.
 }
\]
The variable $\sigma_{11}$ is silent.  The eight RHC extremizers are described by
\[
 \sigma_{13}=\sigma_{17}=\sigma_{19}=\sigma_{23}=\tau,
 \qquad
 \sigma_5=\sigma_7=\tau\sigma_3,
\]
with $\tau,\sigma_3,\sigma_{11}\in\{\pm1\}$.

The first multiplicative dependence among the active endpoint monomials appears at $N=65$:
\[
 \boxed{
 f(33)f(39)f(55)f(65)=1.
 }
\]

\subsection{Exact center of the finite sign cube}

Equip the free odd-prime signs with the uniform product measure.

\begin{proposition}
For $N\ge2$,
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E S_N=0.}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E C_h=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,& h\text{ odd and squarefree, }2h\le N,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 }
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The terms $x_1=1$ and $x_2=-1$ cancel in expectation.  Every other squarefree $n$ contains at least one free odd prime, so $\mathbb E x_n=0$.

For $\mathbb E[x_nx_{n+h}]$ to be nonzero, every free odd prime must occur an even number of times across the two squarefree integers.  Hence $n$ and $n+h$ must have the same set of odd prime divisors.  Since they are distinct squarefree integers, they can then differ only by a factor of $2$.  As $n+h>n$, necessarily $n=h$ and $n+h=2h$, with $h$ odd squarefree.  In that case $x_hx_{2h}=-1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Short correlations and the first witness}

Define
\[
 C_h(x;N)=\sum_{n\le N-h}x_nx_{n+h},
 \qquad 1\le h<N,
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 d_h(x;N)=C_h(x;N)-C_h(\mu;N).
 }
\]
The first positive witness is
\[
 \boxed{
 h_*(x;N)=\min\{h\ge1:d_h(x;N)>0\},
 }
\]
when it exists.

All admissible words have the same zero support as $\mu$, so
\[
 S_x(N)^2=\sum_{n\le N}x_n^2+
 2\sum_{h=1}^{N-1}C_h(x;N).
\]
Subtracting the M\"obius identity gives
\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{h=1}^{N-1}d_h(x;N)
 =\frac{S_x(N)^2-M(N)^2}{2}.
 }
\]
Thus $|S_x(N)|>|M(N)|$ forces at least one positive $d_h$.  The finite combinatorial question is the position of the first such $h$.

For a state with witness $h_*$ define
\[
 D^-(x)=-\sum_{h<h_*}d_h(x)\ge0,
 \qquad
 J(x)=d_{h_*}(x)>0.
\]
These are exact descriptive quantities; $D^-$ alone does not determine $h_*$, as the $N=110$ data below show.

\section{Signed displacement cuts}

\subsection{Cut identity}

Let
\[
 V_N=\{n\le N:\mu(n)\ne0\}.
\]
For each $h$, form the graph
\[
 G_h(N)=(V_N,E_h,w_h),
\]
where
\[
 E_h=\{\{n,n+h\}:1\le n\le N-h,\ n,n+h\in V_N\}
\]
and
\[
 w_h(\{n,n+h\})=-\mu(n)\mu(n+h)\in\{\pm1\}.
\]
For an admissible completion $x$, define its discrepancy set
\[
 \boxed{
 D(x)=\{n\in V_N:x_n\ne\mu(n)\}.
 }
\]
Because the zero support is fixed,
\[
 x_n=
 \begin{cases}
 -\mu(n),&n\in D(x),\\
 \mu(n),&n\notin D(x).
 \end{cases}
\]

Let $\partial_hD$ be the set of edges in $E_h$ with exactly one endpoint in $D$, and define the signed cut
\[
 \Cut_h(D)=\sum_{e\in\partial_hD}w_h(e).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Signed displacement-cut identity]
For every admissible completion and every $1\le h<N$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{C_h(x)-C_h(\mu)}2
 =\Cut_h(D(x)).
 }
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
 A_h(x)=\#\{e\in\partial_hD:w_h(e)=+1\},
 \qquad
 B_h(x)=\#\{e\in\partial_hD:w_h(e)=-1\},
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{
 d_h(x)=2(A_h(x)-B_h(x))
 }
\]
and
\[
 \boxed{
 h_*(x)=\min\{h:A_h(x)>B_h(x)\}.
 }
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If both endpoints of an edge are simultaneously flipped, or neither is flipped, the product $x_nx_{n+h}$ equals $\mu(n)\mu(n+h)$.  If exactly one endpoint is flipped, the product changes sign, so
\[
 x_nx_{n+h}-\mu(n)\mu(n+h)
 =-2\mu(n)\mu(n+h)=2w_h(e).
\]
Summing over crossing edges proves the identity.
\end{proof}

For every $h$, the underlying graph is a disjoint union of fragments of arithmetic progressions
\[
 r,\ r+h,\ r+2h,\ldots,
\]
hence a forest of paths.  If $h>N/2$, every component contains at most one edge, so $G_h$ is a matching.

\subsection{Multiplicative admissibility as a binary code}

Index the free odd primes by $p$.  Write
\[
 b_p=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\sigma_p=-1,\\
 1,&\sigma_p=+1.
 \end{cases}
\]
For squarefree $n$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbf1_{D(x)}(n)
 \equiv\sum_{\substack{p\mid n\\p\ {\rm odd}}}b_p
 \pmod2.
 }
\]
If $A$ is the binary incidence matrix
\[
 A_{n,p}=\mathbf1_{p\mid n}\pmod2,
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{\mathbf1_D=Ab\pmod2.}
\]
Thus admissible cuts are not arbitrary cuts of $G_h$; they belong to the image of a specific binary linear code generated by prime divisibility.

For an edge $e=\{n,n+h\}$ define
\[
 \boxed{
 q_e=A_n+A_{n+h}\in\Ftwo^{\pi(N)-1}.
 }
\]
Then
\[
 e\in\partial_hD
 \iff q_e\cdot b=1\pmod2.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Parity of the edge sign]
For every displacement edge,
\[
 \boxed{
 w_h(e)=(-1)^{|q_e|+h+1},
 }
\]
where $|q_e|$ is the Hamming weight of $q_e$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For squarefree endpoints,
\[
 \mu(n)\mu(n+h)=(-1)^{\omega(n)+\omega(n+h)}.
\]
Every common odd prime contributes twice and disappears modulo $2$; the remaining odd-prime parity is $|q_e|$.  The prime $2$ lies in the symmetric difference of the prime supports exactly when $n$ and $n+h$ have opposite parity, equivalently when $h$ is odd.  Hence
\[
 \omega(n)+\omega(n+h)\equiv |q_e|+h\pmod2,
\]
and multiplying by the leading minus sign gives the formula.
\end{proof}

Group edges with the same parity form:
\[
 m_h(q)=\#\{e\in E_h:q_e=q\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{d_h(b)}2
 =
 \sum_{\substack{q\\q\cdot b=1}}
 m_h(q)(-1)^{|q|+h+1}.
 }
\]
Therefore
\[
 \boxed{
 h_*(b)=
 \min\left\{
 h:
 \sum_{q\cdot b=1}
 m_h(q)(-1)^{|q|+h+1}>0
 \right\}.
 }
\]
This is an exact finite threshold system of parity tests on the prime-sign cube.

\section{Canonical finite benchmark for $1\le N\le 25$}

This section fixes a single reproducible finite benchmark. All counts are exact. The unrestricted ternary layer is computed by coefficient convolution of $(z^{-1}+1+z)^N$; the squarefree-support layer fixes $\mu(1)=1$, fixes zero on nonsquarefree indices, and leaves the remaining squarefree signs free; the multiplicative layer assigns free signs to the primes $p\le N$ and extends multiplicatively on squarefree integers; the last nonterminal layer additionally fixes only $\mu(2)=-1$. No true values $M(N)$ are used in these four layers.

The strict endpoint classes used here are
\[
 \RHC_N=\{\omega:|S_\omega(N)|>\sqrt N\},
 \qquad

\subsection{Immediate finite observations}
The benchmark separates reduction in state count from reduction in the extremal envelope. The short-correlation benchmark adds a different kind of information: the first three correlations identify the true word uniquely among the $256$ fixed-$\sigma_2$ states, and lag $2$ separates it from all eight RHC states.

\section{Bibliographic assessment of the signed-cut formulation}

The identity
\[
 d_h/2=\Cut_h(D)
\]
is elementary once the discrepancy set is introduced, and signed cuts themselves are classical \cite{Zaslavsky1982,Gallier2016}.  Likewise, finite aperiodic autocorrelation is classical \cite{Willms2013}.  The multiplicative-function literature studies correlations and sign patterns by analytic and probabilistic methods \cite{MRT2016,KoukSound2020,AymoneSidoravicius2017}.

The combination used here is more specific:
\[
 \mathcal D_H(N)=
 \left\{
 D=Ab:
 \Cut_h(D)\le0,\ 1\le h\le H
 \right\},
\]
possibly intersected with an endpoint class such as RHC.  Equivalently, on the free prime-sign cube,
\[
 \mathcal B_H(N)=
 \left\{
 b:
 \sum_{q\cdot b=1}
 m_h(q)(-1)^{|q|+h+1}\le0,\
 1\le h\le H
 \right\}.
\]
The nested filtration
\[
 \mathcal B_1\supseteq\mathcal B_2\supseteq\cdots
\]
and the first positive threshold
\[
 h_*(b)=
 \min\left\{
 h:
 \sum_{q\cdot b=1}m_h(q)(-1)^{|q|+h+1}>0
 \right\}
\]
are the combinatorial objects measured by the exhaustive computation.

No exact antecedent for this simultaneous M\"obius-signed displacement-cut filtration, restricted to the multiplicative squarefree completion code, was located in the literature searches performed for this work.  The statement is deliberately narrower than a claim that signed cuts, autocorrelation, parity functions, or multiplicative correlations are new.

\section{Conclusions: original, proved, and useful content}

Within the bibliography surveyed here, the original contribution supported by complete proofs and exact computation is the following finite combinatorial package.

First, the short-correlation comparison against the true M\"obius word is represented exactly as a simultaneous signed-cut problem:
\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{C_h(x)-C_h(\mu)}2
 =\Cut_{G_h}(D(x)).
 }
\]
The admissible discrepancy sets are not arbitrary cuts; they form the binary code image
\[
 \boxed{\mathbf1_D=Ab\pmod2}
\]
generated by odd-prime divisibility.  Every displacement edge is activated by a parity equation
\[
 q_e\cdot b=1,
\]
and its sign is determined internally by
\[
 \boxed{
 -\mu(n)\mu(n+h)=(-1)^{|q_e|+h+1}.
 }
\]
Consequently the first witness is an explicit threshold of weighted parity tests,
\[
 \boxed{
 h_*(b)=
 \min\left\{
 h:
 \sum_{q\cdot b=1}
 m_h(q)(-1)^{|q|+h+1}>0
 \right\}.
 }
\]
No exact antecedent for this simultaneous displacement-cut filtration under multiplicative admissibility was found in the targeted literature search.

Second, the exhaustive $N=110$ instance is completely resolved for the tested family:
\[
 2^{28}=268{,}435{,}456
\]
states,
\[
 9{,}311{,}936
\]
RHC states, and the exact final survivor chain
\[
 \boxed{13\to2\to0}
\]
at lags $9,10,11$, with
\[
 \boxed{H_{\max,0}(110)=11.}
\]
The two final states, their complete $109$-lag cut profiles, and the complete parity-form statistics for every lag are given in the tables above.  These data are not used to assert an asymptotic law; their value is that they expose the finite object to be explained combinatorially.

The qualified novelty claim of this paper is therefore the simultaneous signed displacement-cut formulation restricted to the multiplicative M\"obius completion code, together with its exact parity-threshold reduction and complete finite $N=110$ resolution.
